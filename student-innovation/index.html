About the Book

This volume brings together conceptual frameworks, empirical studies, and field-tested case accounts that position students as innovators, change agents, and co-creators of sustainable solutions at local and global scales. While existing literature has largely examined service-learning and sustainability education as distinct areas, relatively few studies provide a consolidated perspective on how university outreach, experiential learning, and SDG-oriented innovation intersect. This edited volume, drawing on invited contributors from diverse regions and institutional contexts, seeks to address this gap.

The volume moves from the conceptual and pedagogical foundations of university community partnerships to campus living laboratories, human empowerment, climate resilience, and the scaling of student-led innovation. By combining theoretical perspectives with global case studies and an inclusive-growth lens for evaluating impact, the book aims to provide a balanced account of both successful and challenging experiences, including issues of power asymmetries, technological solutionism, institutional constraints, and barriers to scaling. It further seeks to develop a global framework for accountable and reciprocal university–community engagement in support of the 2030 Agenda and the Sustainable Development Goals.

Invited Contributions

We welcome empirical case studies, comparative analyses, conceptual papers, evaluation studies and reflective practitioner accounts. Contributions from faculty, researchers, students, community partners and policy practitioners are encouraged, and chapters that critically examine failure, scaling barriers or unintended consequences are particularly welcome. Preference will be given to contributions involving international co-authorship and collaboration across institutions and geographical regions.

Submission Guidelines

  1. Font and Size: Times New Roman, 12-point font
  2. Line Spacing: 1.5
  3. Referencing Style: APA 7th Edition
  4. Table captions should be placed above the respective tables.
  5. Figure captions should be placed below the respective figures.
  6. Chapter Abstract (in the full chapter): Maximum 250 words
  7. Word Limit: Maximum 6000 words
  8. Chapters should be free from grammatical and typographical errors, and should be carefully proofread before submission.
  9. At least 70% of the cited references should be from the most recent five years.
  10. All in-text citations must be accurately cross-checked against the reference list.

All submissions will undergo double-blind peer review. Chapters must be original, unpublished and not under consideration elsewhere. Similarity index should be below 10% and AI-generated content below 20%.
